A field guide to the district

I'm New Here

LACCD is nine colleges, several major systems, a district office, local campus offices, and enough acronyms to make a perfectly competent person wonder whether everyone else received a secret handbook.

You did not miss the secret handbook. This is the closest thing.

Yes, you can ask that question. Someone else is also pretending they already know.

Do not learn the whole district tonight

Start with these five things

If you were handed a class and very little else, these are the things that matter first. The rest can wait.

1

Find your chair and department contact

Your chair or department office is usually the fastest route to local answers: room access, textbooks, copying, absence procedures, course materials, and who does what on your campus.

Find your English DDC contact among The 9

2

Make sure your LACCD login works

Your LACCD credentials open the doors to PeopleSoft/SIS, Office 365 email, Canvas and other employee tools. If the login itself fails, start with your college IT office.

Open the LACCD SIS login help page

3

Confirm the class in SIS and Canvas

PeopleSoft/SIS is the official student-record system. Canvas is the teaching space. Make sure the section, roster, dates, modality, meeting pattern and Canvas shell are the ones you expected.

Open MyCollege / PeopleSoft SIS

4

Get the current course outline

The Course Outline of Record (COR) is the approved course: catalog description, objectives/outcomes, content, methods and other required elements. Ask your department for the current COR; LACCD uses eLumen for curriculum management.

See LACCD curriculum support and eLumen

5

Ask the boring logistical questions now

Parking. ID. Keys. Classroom technology. Copying/printing. Mailbox. Campus emergency number. What to do if you are absent. None of this is self-evident, and much of it is college-specific.

If you only remember one thing

Districtwide systems and college procedures are not the same thing. Your SIS login may follow you across LACCD; your parking permit, copy code, room key, local Canvas support, payroll contact, FLEX process, and absence procedure may not.

Four doors you will open a lot

Know the systems

PeopleSoft / SIS

Think: official records. Your class assignment, roster, add authorizations, census/attendance-related roster work, and grade rosters live here. It is the system of record for LACCD student administration.

Open MyCollege / SIS

Canvas

Think: teaching. Course pages, assignments, announcements, grades students see, and online course activity. District faculty accessibility resources also live around the Canvas ecosystem.

Office 365 / LACCD email

Think: official communication. Check this account regularly. District and college notices, DSPS accommodation notifications, HR messages, and department communication may land there.

Vision Resource Center (VRC)

Think: professional learning and FLEX. Colleges use the VRC for training and professional-development activity. Your exact FLEX obligation depends on assignment and current rules, so verify it with your college.

Why do I have two places with grades?

Canvas is the classroom gradebook you manage during the term. SIS is the official record. At the end of the course, do not assume Canvas has submitted final grades for you.

The semester has pressure points

Your first-semester timeline

Before the first class

  • Confirm the official section number, dates, modality, meeting days/times, room or online format, and enrollment in SIS.
  • Confirm you can access the correct Canvas shell and that students will be able to find what they need when the course opens.
  • Get the current COR and any department syllabus template or required course-specific language.
  • Confirm the textbook/OER/ZTC plan and whether anything must be ordered or placed on reserve.
  • Find your college's process for parking, employee ID, keys/card access, copying, classroom technology and urgent IT support.
  • Ask who to contact if illness or an emergency prevents you from teaching. Do not independently hire or arrange a substitute.

First class and first week

  • Keep attendance/participation records from the beginning. For standard census courses, early attendance matters for the official roster process.
  • Know how add authorizations work in SIS before promising a seat to a student.
  • Check that students who believe they added the course actually appear on the official roster.
  • Read your LACCD email. Accommodation notices and other time-sensitive messages may arrive there.
  • Tell students how to reach you and where they can find current course information. You do not need a private office to be an accessible instructor.

Before census

  • Do not guess the deadline. Census and other important dates depend on the class. Check the section's Important Class Dates / roster information in SIS.
  • Complete the required census/roster process and deal with no-shows or nonparticipation according to current college/district procedure.
  • Keep documentation that supports roster decisions. Enrollment and attendance reporting affect both students and state apportionment.

During the term

  • Acknowledge and provide approved DSPS accommodations. If an accommodation is unclear, contact the student's campus DSPS office rather than asking the student to disclose a diagnosis.
  • Use your campus student-support ecosystem: tutoring, library, basic needs, counseling, DSPS, EOPS, Veterans, Dream/undocumented-student support, and other programs. You do not have to personally solve every problem a student brings you.
  • Keep your own grade and participation records, especially for decisions that may later need explanation.
  • Complete required professional-development/FLEX activity and reporting for your assignment; ask your local professional-development office how your college handles it.

End of the course

  • Submit official final grades in SIS by the deadline supplied for your section/college.
  • Do not rely on the Canvas gradebook as the official submission.
  • Keep the records required by your college and current contract/policy in case a grade question or evaluation arises.
  • If you hope to teach again, stay in contact with your department and learn how adjunct seniority/reemployment works under the current Faculty Guild agreement.

The acronym fog

Decode the things people say as if you were born knowing them

LACCD
Los Angeles Community College District: the district connecting all nine colleges.
SIS / PeopleSoft
The Student Information System. Official rosters, enrollment and grade records live here.
SSO
Single sign-on: your district credentials used to reach multiple LACCD systems.
SAP
The district's enterprise system for employee/human-capital and other administrative data. You may hear HR refer to something being “in SAP.”
ESC
Educational Services Center: the LACCD district office at 770 Wilshire Boulevard.
COR
Course Outline of Record: the officially approved academic blueprint for a course.
eLumen
LACCD's curriculum management system.
SLO
Student Learning Outcome. Course outcomes are part of assessment and program improvement work.
VRC
Vision Resource Center: professional learning and, at many colleges, FLEX tracking.
FLEX
Professional-development activity tied to the flexible academic calendar. Adjunct obligations depend on teaching load/current rules; verify locally.
DSPS
Disabled Student Programs and Services: the campus office that determines and coordinates approved student accommodations.
OER / ZTC
Open Educational Resources / Zero Textbook Cost.
DDC
District Discipline Committee: faculty from the nine colleges discussing discipline-level district matters.
DAS
District Academic Senate: districtwide faculty voice on academic and professional matters.
DCC
District Curriculum Committee.
CCN
Common Course Numbering. California is changing many high-enrollment course numbers/titles in phases; ENGL C1000 is one result.
DE
Usually Distance Education in faculty conversations. “DE certification” normally means authorization/training to teach online. For dual enrollment, it is clearer to say dual enrollment.
AFT 1521 / Faculty Guild
Los Angeles College Faculty Guild, the union representing LACCD faculty.

You are faculty, full stop

Adjunct things worth knowing early

Belonging is not the same as unlimited unpaid labor

You are welcome at department, college and district spaces. You also get to ask whether an activity is paid, carries FLEX credit, has a stipend, or is purely voluntary. Participation should not be a secret test of whether you “care enough.”

  • You are covered by the faculty collective-bargaining agreement. The contract covers matters such as assignments, pay, evaluations, working conditions, leaves, distance education and adjunct seniority/reemployment.
  • Check current contract information rather than an old PDF. As of this guide's September 2026 review, the public District and Guild pages still point to the July 2023 to June 2026 agreement while successor bargaining is underway. Use the Guild's current bargaining page or ask your campus chapter for the latest status.
  • Adjunct health benefits exist, but eligibility rules matter. LACCD's Health Benefits Unit has a specific adjunct pathway. Do not assume “part-time means no benefits”; check your current load and eligibility directly with the Benefits Unit.
  • Learn seniority/reemployment before you need it. The posted agreement contains detailed adjunct seniority provisions. Ask the Guild or your chair how your discipline list, qualifying semesters and assignment history are being recorded under the current agreement.
  • Evaluation is normal. Adjunct faculty are part of the faculty evaluation system. Ask your chair when you should expect evaluation and what materials/process your college uses.
  • Teaching at more than one LACCD college is normal. Some district systems follow you; local processes do not. Keep a tiny campus-specific note for each college: chair, HR/payroll, IT, parking/keys, DE support, FLEX contact and absence procedure.

Freeway flying is already enough logistics. The bureaucracy does not also get to be a personality test.

Make yourself a six-line campus cheat sheet

  • Chair / department contact: who answers ordinary teaching questions?
  • HR / payroll: who fixes an assignment or paycheck problem?
  • IT / online learning: who fixes login, room tech or Canvas problems?
  • Access: where do you get parking, ID, keys/card access and printing?
  • Professional development: who explains your FLEX obligation and reporting?
  • Absence: who must you contact if you cannot teach?

The humane troubleshooting tree

When something goes sideways

I cannot log in

Start with your college IT/help desk. LACCD's SSO page also tells faculty/staff with sign-in problems to contact College IT.

My class is missing or wrong in Canvas

First confirm the assignment in SIS. Then contact your college's Canvas/Distance Education support or IT. A Canvas problem may begin upstream with the official assignment.

My paycheck or assignment looks wrong

Start with campus HR/Payroll. If the issue is about contractual rights or is not getting resolved, contact your AFT 1521 campus chapter.

A student gives me an accommodation letter

Follow the approved accommodation. If you are uncertain how to implement it, contact campus DSPS. Do not ask the student to prove or explain the diagnosis to you.

I do not know the census/drop deadline

Look at the official dates for your exact section in SIS. Short-term and other sections can have different deadlines from the full-semester calendar.

I am going to miss class

Contact your chair/department using your college's absence procedure. Do not independently arrange a substitute unless the college directs you to do so.

I need the official course requirements

Ask for the current COR. For curriculum-system questions, LACCD's EPIE office identifies eLumen as the District curriculum management system.

I do not know who the right person is

Ask your chair, your English DDC representative, or another English colleague. “Who owns this?” is a completely legitimate LACCD question.

Questions you are absolutely allowed to ask

  • “Could you point me to the current COR for this course?”
  • “Where do I find the important dates for my exact section?”
  • “What is our campus procedure for parking, keys/card access, printing and classroom technology?”
  • “Who handles Canvas support here?”
  • “What is my FLEX obligation for this assignment, and where do I report it?”
  • “If I am sick and cannot teach, who do I contact and what is the substitute process?”
  • “Is this meeting paid, FLEX-eligible, stipend-supported, or voluntary?”
  • “I teach this course at another LACCD college too. Is this procedure districtwide or local?”

One more thing

You are not visiting.

Adjunct does not mean peripheral. You teach LACCD students, which makes you part of the faculty community. You do not have to earn belonging by already knowing the systems, volunteering for everything, or being physically on campus all day.

Ask the question. Find the person. Take what helps. Share when you want to.
